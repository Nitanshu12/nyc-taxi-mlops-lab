# 003: Ingress scope

Stage: S0 · Date: 2026-11-21 · Author: Nitanshu12 · Drafted by agent: yes

## Choice

Port 80 on the app host (Traefik) is open to exactly two addresses: my public IPv4 and the grader's,
`/32` each (`ingress_cidrs` in the gitignored `infra/live/terraform.tfvars`). Nothing else on any
host is reachable from the internet, and there is no port 22.

## Rejected alternative

`0.0.0.0/0`, the whole internet. It never locks me out and works from any network, but:
- the app has no login, HTTPS, rate limit or firewall, so anyone could call `/predict`;
- internet scanners find open port-80 hosts within minutes, and their junk requests would land in
  the app's request and error numbers, which later stages measure (SLOs, canary comparisons);
- nobody besides me and the grader needs to reach it: the app's traffic is mine, not real riders'.

## Why

- All the traffic the app gets is generated by me or the grader. The given S8 load generator
  (`deploy/traffic.py`) runs on the app host against `localhost` and needs no rule; load I send from
  my laptop (e.g. Locust) comes from my IP, which is on the list.
- Nothing in the pipeline needs inbound access: the runner and SSM connect outward, CD's smoke test
  calls `127.0.0.1` on the host, and host-to-host traffic is allowed by security group, not by IP.
- Smallest exposure for an app with no protection of its own.

Trade-off accepted: when my IP changes (another Wi-Fi, a hotspot, a VPN, a router restart), I am
locked out until I update `terraform.tfvars` and apply. That only adds or removes port-80 rules; the
host and its Elastic IP stay the same.

## What would show this was wrong

- I get locked out often enough that updating my IP costs more time than it saves.
- The grader can't reach the app (wrong or changed grader IP).
- Someone else needs access (a classmate, a demo audience).
- Load has to come from somewhere with changing addresses (GitHub-hosted runners, a cloud
  load-testing service), which a `/32` list can't cover.
